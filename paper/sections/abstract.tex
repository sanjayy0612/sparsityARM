\begin{abstract}
Dynamic feed-forward-network (FFN) sparsity skips neurons that contribute
little to the current token, but on CPUs the irregular memory access of
neuron-level masks can erase the expected speedup. A common compromise is to
skip fixed contiguous \emph{blocks} of neurons. We ask whether block sparsity
can both preserve model quality and beat an optimized dense baseline on an
Apple M2 CPU, measuring the two separately: how much oracle-selected block
sparsity a model tolerates under a pre-specified 5\% relative-perplexity gate,
and how fast the resulting masks execute against Apple Accelerate. Blocks of
eight neurons (B8) passed the gate only up to 10\% sparsity for Llama 3.2 1B
and 20\% for TinyLlama 1.1B. A packed B8 executor was 27--28\% slower than
dense at the passing Llama point and first won only at 40--50\% sparsity, so it
has no quality--speed intersection. The same block-aligned masks run through a
simple per-neuron executor with neuron-major weights, however, were faster
than dense at the quality ceiling: by 0.9--3.5\% on real Llama masks and
2.6--7.3\% (Llama) and 5.1--7.3\% (TinyLlama) on synthetic ones. Block
alignment therefore helps, but through memory layout rather than packed block
kernels, and the quality ceiling caps the achievable gain at 11\% and 25\% of
FFN time even for a perfect kernel. Thirteen supporting experiments on
layouts, SIMD, coalescing, and quantized paths, together with all raw
evidence, are released for reproduction.
\end{abstract}
