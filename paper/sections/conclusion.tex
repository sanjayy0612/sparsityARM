\section{Conclusion}

ARM-Sparse investigated whether contiguous FFN blocks convert dynamic
sparsity into practical Apple M2 CPU speedups. Blocks did accelerate isolated
FFN execution at sufficiently high sparsity. However, Llama 3.2 1B and
TinyLlama 1.1B both crossed their quality limits 30 percentage points before
their first measured speedup points. Under the recorded conditions, block-based
sparsity therefore did not produce a verified quality-preserving wall-clock
gain. The complete negative result, raw artifacts, validators, figures, and
manuscript sources are retained for reproduction and for testing future methods
that explicitly create block-compatible model structure.
