\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Activation and contextual sparsity.}
Li et al.\ observed that trained ReLU transformers naturally exhibit sparse
FFN activations~\cite{lazyneuron}, and MoEfication showed that FFN neurons can be
grouped into expert-like clusters after training~\cite{moefication}. DejaVu
introduced lightweight input-dependent predictors for contextual sparsity in
MLP and attention blocks and demonstrated GPU speedups~\cite{dejavu}. Because
modern models use smooth SwiGLU activations~\cite{glu} rather than ReLU,
later work either restores ReLU through fine-tuning~\cite{relustrikes} or
thresholds activations without training~\cite{cats,teal}. These methods
operate at neuron or channel granularity; ARM-Sparse asks what happens to
quality when that granularity is coarsened into contiguous blocks for CPU
efficiency.

\paragraph{Sparsity-aware systems for constrained devices.}
LLM in a Flash showed that loading bundled rows and columns in larger
contiguous chunks improves flash throughput~\cite{llmflash}, which motivates
our layout hypothesis, although our weights stay in RAM and our objective is
CPU latency. PowerInfer exploits hot/cold neuron locality to split work
between consumer CPUs and GPUs~\cite{powerinfer}, and PowerInfer-2 elevates
neuron clusters to scheduling and storage units on smartphones~\cite{powerinfer2}.
Dynamic Input Pruning jointly studies SwiGLU pruning and cache-aware masking on
resource-constrained hardware~\cite{dip}. Clustered execution is therefore
prior art; our study differs by fixing hardware, mask budgets, and a strong
dense comparator and measuring exactly where block execution crosses both a
quality gate and dense latency.

\paragraph{Static and structured weight sparsity.}
Post-training pruning methods such as SparseGPT~\cite{sparsegpt} and
Wanda~\cite{wanda} remove weights permanently, and hardware-supported 2:4
sparsity~\cite{mishra21} and block-sparse GPU kernels~\cite{blocksparse} show
that structure is usually required before sparsity becomes speed. A broad
survey is given by Hoefler et al.~\cite{hoefler}. ARM-Sparse is dynamic
(masks change per token) and targets a CPU without sparse tensor hardware,
which makes the structure--quality trade-off central.

\paragraph{Performance modelling.}
Our break-even analysis follows the spirit of the roofline
model~\cite{roofline}: at batch size one, FFN execution is a sequence of
matrix--vector products whose cost is governed mainly by how efficiently
weights are streamed from memory.
