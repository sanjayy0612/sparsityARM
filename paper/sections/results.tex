\section{Results}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includesvg[width=0.88\textwidth]{figures/quality-vs-sparsity}
\caption{Relative perplexity increase for output-contribution B8 masks. The
dashed line is the preregistered 5\% gate.}
\label{fig:quality}
\end{figure*}

Llama B8 at 10\% increased perplexity by 2.520\% and passed. At 20\% it
increased by 8.773\% and failed. TinyLlama passed at 10\% and 20\%, with
increases of 1.336\% and 3.157\%, then failed at 30\% with 6.874\%. TinyLlama
B32 passed only at 10\%, showing that larger contiguous units worsened the
quality tradeoff in this screen.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includesvg[width=0.88\textwidth]{figures/speedup-vs-sparsity}
\caption{Mean B8 FFN speedup over Accelerate dense. Points summarize three
runs; raw per-run medians and ranges remain in the result bundles.}
\label{fig:speed}
\end{figure*}

For the Llama geometry, native B8 remained 8.9--11.7\% slower at 30\% and
first became faster at the 40\% grid point, by 1.6--3.1\%. TinyLlama B8
remained 1.1--5.2\% slower at 40\% and first won at 50\%, by 10.7--17.1\%.
The exact crossovers were not interpolated.

At the real-mask Llama B8/10\% quality-compatible condition, packed block
execution was 37.26--38.77\% slower than Accelerate dense. Thus the observed
mechanical speedups do not transfer to the accepted quality region.
